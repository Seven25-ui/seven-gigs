7 Common Mistakes New Freelancers Make on Upwork (and How to Avoid Them)

Oct 25, 2026 · 3 min read

Starting on Upwork can feel slow. You send proposals, wait, and hear nothing. Before you decide the platform doesn't work, check whether you are making one of these common mistakes. Most of them are easy to fix.

1. Sending the same proposal to every job

Clients can spot a copy-paste proposal in seconds. If your message could be sent to any job, it will be ignored by all of them.

The fix: spend two minutes reading the job post and mention something specific from it, such as the client's goal, a tool they use, or a question they asked. Keep the proposal short and make the first two lines about the client's problem, not about you.

2. A vague or incomplete profile

When a client opens your proposal, the next thing they do is look at your profile. A blank photo, a generic title like "Freelancer", and an empty overview make you look risky.

The fix: use a clear title that says what you do (for example "Python Developer for Telegram Bots"), add a friendly photo, and write an overview that explains who you help and how. Add a few portfolio items, even small personal projects.

3. Applying to everything

Most proposals cost Connects, so spraying them at every job wastes your budget and your energy. It also leads to rushed, low-quality applications.

The fix: apply only to jobs where you can honestly say, "I can do this well." Fewer, better proposals almost always beat many weak ones.

4. Pricing yourself too low

New freelancers often charge very little to win the first job. The problem is that low prices attract clients who expect a lot for almost nothing, and it is hard to raise your rates later with the same client.

The fix: research what similar freelancers charge and set a fair rate that you can live with. If you want to start lower to earn your first reviews, set a clear plan to raise your price after a few jobs.

5. Starting without a clear scope

"Just build me a website" can mean ten different things. When the scope is unclear, you end up doing extra work, the client feels disappointed, and both of you argue about what was promised.

The fix: before you start, write down what is included, what is not, how many revisions you offer, and when you will deliver. Ask the client to confirm it. Clear expectations protect both sides.

6. Taking the work or payments off the platform

Some clients will ask you to move to another app and be paid directly. It can be tempting, but it usually breaks Upwork's terms, and it removes the protection you get from the platform if there is a payment problem.

The fix: keep your contract, messages, and payments inside Upwork. You can still use other tools for quick chats if the client prefers, as long as the work and payment stay on the platform.

7. Ignoring red flags

Not every client is a good client. Warning signs include an unclear job description, a request for free sample work, pressure to start before a contract exists, or a payment method that isn't verified.

The fix: trust your instincts. Walking away from one bad job protects your time, your rating, and your peace of mind.

A quick checklist before you hit send

1. Did I mention something specific from the job post?
2. Is my profile complete and clear?
3. Is this job truly a good fit for me?
4. Is my price fair?
5. Do I understand exactly what the client wants?
6. Does the work and payment stay on the platform?

The bottom line

Nobody gets everything right at the start. Pick one mistake from this list, fix it this week, and then move on to the next. Small improvements add up quickly, and your first good review often makes the second client much easier to win.
