How to Ask Clients for Reviews and Testimonials Without Feeling Awkward

Nov 12, 2026 · 3 min read

When you are new, reviews are worth more than almost anything else on your profile. A single good review tells the next client that someone already trusted you and was happy with the result. Yet many freelancers feel awkward asking for one. The good news is that asking is simple when you do it at the right time and in the right way.

1. Ask when the client is happiest

The best moment is right after you deliver the final work and the client says something positive, like "Looks great" or "Thank you." At that point they feel good about the project and the result is fresh in their mind. Waiting a few weeks makes the request feel strange, and the client may forget the details.

2. Make the project end clearly

Before you ask, confirm that everything is finished. Send a short message that lists what you delivered and ask if anything is missing. When the client confirms, close the contract. On Upwork, the review step comes when the contract ends, so a clear ending makes feedback easier to give.

3. Use a short, friendly message

You do not need a long speech. A few honest sentences are enough. Here are two examples you can adjust.

"I am glad you like the result! If you are happy with my work, a short review would help me a lot as I am still building my profile. Thank you again for the chance."

"It was a pleasure working on this. When you have a minute, I would really appreciate honest feedback on the project. It helps me improve and helps other clients know what to expect."

Notice that both messages are polite, short, and do not pressure the client.

4. Never ask for a specific rating

Do not say "please give me five stars." Ask for honest feedback instead. It feels more natural, and it respects the rules of the platform. Also never offer money, discounts, or favors in exchange for a review, and never ask a friend to write a fake one. Those things can get your account in trouble and they destroy trust.

5. What if the client does not answer?

Some clients are busy and simply forget. It is fine to send one gentle reminder a few days later. Keep it light: "Just a quick reminder in case you missed my message. Thank you again!" If there is still no reply, let it go. Pushing more can make the client feel uncomfortable, and one missing review is not the end of the world.

6. Collect testimonials outside the platform too

Besides platform reviews, you can ask happy clients for a short testimonial that you can show on your portfolio or blog. Ask permission first, and ask how they would like their name or company to appear. A short quote of one or two sentences is enough, as long as it is real.

7. Learn from feedback you do not like

Sometimes a client leaves a lower rating or a critical comment. It hurts, but do not argue in public. Reply politely, thank them for the feedback, and think about what you can improve. A calm, professional answer actually makes new clients trust you more.

8. Make asking part of your routine

Add one line to your project checklist: "Ask for feedback after delivery." When it becomes a habit, you will never forget it, and your reviews will grow steadily.

Final thoughts

Reviews are not magic, but they are one of the strongest signals you can build. Do good work, communicate clearly, finish the project properly, and then ask with honesty and respect. Most happy clients are glad to help.
