How to Send a Simple Invoice and Follow Up on Late Payments

Nov 20, 2026 · 3 min read

Getting paid is the part of freelancing nobody enjoys talking about. You finish the work, send it, and then wait. Most clients pay on time, but some forget, some are busy, and a few delay on purpose. The good news is that clear habits at the start prevent most payment problems.

This guide is general information, not legal advice. If you work through a platform like Upwork, use its contract and milestone tools for those clients, and read the platform's current rules. The steps below are mostly for direct clients.

Step 1: Agree on payment before you start

Most late payments begin with an unclear agreement. Before you write any code or any text, confirm these points in writing:

1. The total price, or your rate
2. When you get paid: before you start, in parts, or after delivery
3. Which payment methods you accept
4. How many days the client has to pay after you send the invoice

For small projects, asking for part of the payment first is common. For example, half before you start and half when you deliver. It shows that both sides are serious.

Step 2: Write a simple invoice

An invoice does not need fancy software. A clean document or even a clear message can work. Include:

1. Your name and contact details
2. The client's name
3. An invoice number and the date
4. A short list of what you delivered and the price of each item
5. The total amount and the currency
6. The payment methods and the due date
7. A thank-you line

Give each invoice a number, such as 001, 002, 003. It keeps your records clean and makes it easy to refer to a specific invoice in a message.

Step 3: Send it right after delivery

Do not wait a week. Send the invoice when you deliver the work or when the client approves it, while they feel happy about the result. Add a short message like this:

Hi Anna, the project is delivered. I attached invoice 001. Payment is due within 7 days. Please tell me if you need anything changed. Thank you!

Step 4: Follow up in steps

If the due date passes, stay calm. Most late payments are simple forgetfulness. Follow up in small, polite steps.

Reminder 1, one or two days after the due date:

Hi Anna, a quick reminder that invoice 001 was due on Friday. Could you let me know when I can expect the payment? Thank you!

Reminder 2, about a week later:

Hi Anna, I am following up on invoice 001, which is now a week overdue. Please send the payment or tell me if there is a problem I can help with.

Reminder 3, firmer:

Hi Anna, invoice 001 is still unpaid. Please pay by [date]. After that date I will need to pause any further work for your account.

Keep every message short, polite, and in writing. Never insult the client, even if you are frustrated.

Step 5: Know when to stop work

If a client owes you money, do not keep delivering new work for free. It is fair to pause until the old invoice is paid. Say it calmly and give a clear date.

Step 6: Protect yourself next time

1. Ask for a deposit on new clients
2. Split bigger jobs into smaller payments
3. Do not send the final files until the final payment arrives, when that was agreed
4. Keep all messages and invoices in one folder
5. Choose payment methods that leave a record

When a client never pays

If a client stops answering and the amount is large, check the options for your situation, such as the platform's support process or advice from a qualified person in your country. Do not threaten the client or post about them publicly, because that can hurt you more than them.

The bottom line

Clear terms, a simple invoice, and polite reminders solve most payment problems. Agree on the rules first, send the invoice on time, and follow up without drama. You are running a small business, and a calm routine around money keeps it healthy.
