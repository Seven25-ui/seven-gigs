How to Find Better Jobs on Upwork With Filters and Saved Searches

Nov 12, 2026 · 3 min read

Many beginners open Upwork, scroll through hundreds of jobs, and send proposals to almost anything. That wastes Connects and energy. A smarter way is to build a simple search routine that shows you only the jobs worth your time. Here is a practical approach you can start today.

1. Search with specific keywords

Do not search for broad words like "developer" or "AI." Use the exact work you can do well. For example: "n8n automation," "Telegram bot," "chatbot FAQ," "Google Sheets automation," or "Make.com workflow." Specific keywords show jobs where your skills are a real match.

2. Try different words for the same job

Clients do not always use the same terms. One might write "workflow automation" and another "connect my apps." Keep a short list of keywords and try each one. You may find jobs that others miss.

3. Use the filters

Upwork gives you filters that help you remove weak jobs. The names can change over time, but these are usually available:

- Job type: fixed price or hourly, depending on what you prefer
- Experience level: entry level is usually the best place to start
- Number of proposals: lower numbers mean less competition
- Client history: clients with past hires and a verified payment method are safer
- Budget or hourly range: skip jobs that are clearly too low for the work

4. Sort by newest

Early proposals have a better chance of being seen. After you search, sort by the newest jobs and check them first. Jobs posted in the last hour or two with only a few proposals are often the best use of your Connects.

5. Save your searches

When you find a search that works, save it. This lets you open the same results again in one click, and some saved searches can notify you when new jobs appear. Create a few saved searches, for example one for automation, one for chatbots, and one for small website fixes.

6. Pick a fixed time to check

Instead of staying on Upwork all day, choose two or three short times to check, like morning and evening. This keeps your energy up and stops you from sending rushed proposals out of boredom.

7. Spend two minutes reading each job

Before you open the proposal box, quickly check:

- Is the job clear and realistic?
- Is the payment method verified?
- How many proposals are already in?
- Can I really do this work well?

If any answer is "no," skip it. A few good proposals beat many weak ones.

8. Track your proposals

Make a simple sheet with the job title, date, Connects used, and result. After a few weeks you will see which kinds of jobs give you replies, and which ones waste your time. Use that information to adjust your keywords.

9. Improve as you learn

If a certain kind of job keeps ignoring you, change your approach. Maybe you need a better portfolio sample, a shorter proposal, or a narrower focus. Treat your search as an experiment that gets better every week.

Final thoughts

Finding good jobs is a skill, and you can practice it. With specific keywords, smart filters, saved searches, and a steady routine, you will spend fewer Connects and send better proposals. The result is a calmer job hunt and a better chance of landing your first client.
