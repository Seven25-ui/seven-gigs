How to Use AI to Research a Client Before You Pitch

Oct 26, 2026 · 4 min read

Most proposals talk about the freelancer. The ones that win talk about the client. When you show that you understand their business, their customers, and their problem, you instantly stand apart.

The problem is time. Researching every client by hand can take an hour. AI can help you do it in a few minutes, as long as you use it carefully.

What to look for

Before you write a proposal, try to answer these questions:

1. What does the client's business do, and who are its customers?
2. What exactly are they asking for, and what problem is behind it?
3. What tone does the brand use: formal, friendly, playful?
4. Who are similar businesses, and what do they do well?
5. What questions would make you look thoughtful?

Step 1: Start with the job post

Read the post carefully first. The best clues are in it: the words the client uses, the tools they mention, and any problem they describe.

Then ask AI to help you read it more deeply.

Sample prompt: "Here is a job post: [paste it]. What is the real problem the client is trying to solve? List what is clearly stated, what is implied, and what is missing."

Step 2: Collect public information

Look at what the client has made public: their website, their social media pages, and any public description of the business. Copy the useful text, such as the About page or the product descriptions.

Only use information that is public. Don't try to find private details about people, and don't pretend to know things you could only have learned by digging into someone's personal life.

Step 3: Ask AI to summarize

Paste what you collected and ask for a short summary.

Sample prompt: "Here is text from a company's website: [paste it]. In five bullet points, tell me what the company does, who its customers are, and what tone it uses."

Some AI tools can search the web, but they may make mistakes or mix up companies with similar names. Giving the tool the text yourself is more reliable.

Step 4: Find smart questions

Good questions show you think like a professional, and they start a conversation.

Sample prompt: "Based on this job post and company summary, suggest five thoughtful questions a freelancer could ask before starting the project. Focus on goals, audience, and success measures."

Choose one or two of the best questions. Don't send a long list in your first message.

Step 5: Use it in your opening lines

Now turn the research into the first two lines of your proposal. They should be about the client, not about you.

Weak: "I am an experienced developer and I can do this job."

Better: "I saw that your shop sells handmade soap and most of your visitors come from their phones. A faster mobile checkout is probably the quickest way to get more orders, and here is how I would start."

The second one proves you did your homework.

Check before you send

1. Verify every fact. AI can get names, numbers, and details wrong.
2. Remove anything you can't prove or are not sure about.
3. Don't sound like a stalker. Mention the business, not private details.
4. Keep your own voice. Edit the AI's wording until it sounds like you.

A prompt template to copy

Here is a job post: [paste].
Here is public text from the client's website: [paste].
1. Summarize what the business does and who its customers are.
2. Describe the real problem behind the job post.
3. Suggest three questions I could ask the client.
4. Suggest one opening line for my proposal that focuses on the client's problem.
Do not invent facts. If something is unknown, say so.

The last line matters. It reminds the tool not to make things up.

The bottom line

AI can turn an hour of research into ten minutes, but it can't replace your judgment. Read the job post yourself, use only public information, check the facts, and write the proposal in your own words. A short message that proves you understand the client is worth more than a long, polished one that could be sent to anyone.
