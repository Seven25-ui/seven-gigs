How to Write Better AI Prompts: A Simple Formula for Freelancers

Oct 26, 2026 · 3 min read

Most people type a short sentence into an AI tool, get a generic answer, and decide the tool isn't very good. In reality, the answer is only as good as the instructions you give.

The good news is that you don't need any special tricks. You need a simple habit: give the AI enough to work with. Here is a formula that works in ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini, and most other chat tools.

The formula: RTCFC

Think of five parts. You don't always need all five, but the more you include, the better the result.

1. Role: who the AI should act as
2. Task: what you want it to do
3. Context: the background it needs to know
4. Format: how you want the answer to look
5. Constraints: what to include or avoid

A weak prompt versus a strong one

Weak prompt:

"Write a message to my client about a delay."

That is too vague. The AI doesn't know who the client is, why there is a delay, or what tone to use.

Strong prompt:

"You are a professional freelance web developer. Write a short message to a client explaining that their website will be delivered two days late because I'm waiting for their logo files. Be polite and calm, offer a new delivery date of Friday, and ask them to send the files today. Keep it under 100 words. Do not use apologies like 'I deeply regret'. Write it in a friendly, natural tone."

Here is how it follows the formula:

1. Role: freelance web developer
2. Task: write a message about a delay
3. Context: waiting for the logo files, new date is Friday
4. Format: short message under 100 words
5. Constraints: polite, no over-the-top apologies, natural tone

The second prompt takes thirty more seconds to write, and saves you many minutes of fixing a bad answer.

Five habits that make a big difference

1. Give an example of your style. Paste a short message you wrote before and say, "Match this tone." The AI will sound much more like you.
2. Say what to avoid. A "do not use" list removes stiff phrases like "I am excited to" before they appear.
3. Ask it to ask you questions. Try: "Before you write, ask me any questions you need to do this well." This helps when you aren't sure what details matter.
4. Ask for options. "Give me three versions: formal, friendly, and very short." Then choose the best one or mix them.
5. Improve in steps. Don't expect perfection on the first try. Follow up with "Make it shorter", "Make the first line stronger", or "Remove anything that sounds generic."

A prompt template to copy

Replace the parts in brackets:

You are a [role]. I need you to [task].
Background: [context about the client, project, or situation].
Format: [length, structure, tone].
Please avoid: [phrases, styles, or topics you don't want].
Before you start, ask me any questions you need.

Save this template in a notes app, and you can use it again and again.

Where freelancers can use it

1. Writing proposals and follow-up messages
2. Summarizing a long job post or brief
3. Brainstorming ideas, titles, and outlines
4. Explaining a technical topic in simple words
5. Checking your writing for clarity and tone

Be careful

1. Check the facts. AI can sound sure and still be wrong. Verify any names, numbers, or claims before sending them to a client.
2. Protect private information. Don't paste confidential client data into an AI tool unless you are allowed to.
3. Keep your own voice. Treat the answer as a draft. Edit it until it sounds like you.

The bottom line

Better prompts come from giving better instructions. Tell the AI who it is, what to do, what it needs to know, how to format the answer, and what to avoid. Practice with real tasks from your own work, and you'll soon get answers you can actually use.
