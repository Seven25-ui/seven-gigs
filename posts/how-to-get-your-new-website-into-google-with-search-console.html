How to Get Your New Website Into Google With Search Console

Nov 16, 2026 · 4 min read

After you publish a website, the next question is simple: how do people find it on Google? Google will find many sites on its own, but you can speed things up and see what is happening by using Google Search Console. It is free, and it shows you which pages are known to Google, which pages have problems, and what people search for before they visit your site. This guide shows the basic steps for a new website.

Before you start

Make sure your site is online with HTTPS, and that it has a few real pages: a home page, some posts or content, an About page, and a Contact page. If you have a sitemap file, such as sitemap.xml, that makes the next steps easier. A sitemap is a list of the pages on your site that helps Google discover them.

Step 1: Add your site as a property

Go to Search Console and sign in with your Google account. Click Add property. You will see two choices:

- Domain: covers your whole domain, including subdomains and both http and https. This is a good choice if you own your own domain name.
- URL prefix: covers one exact address only, for example https://example.com/. This is useful if you cannot change your DNS settings.

Choose Domain if you can, then type your domain name without https.

Step 2: Verify that you own the site

Google needs proof that the site is yours. For a Domain property, Search Console shows you a TXT record. Open the DNS settings at the company where you bought your domain, add a new TXT record, use @ as the name, and paste the value that Google gave you. Save it, then go back to Search Console and click Verify.

DNS changes can take a few minutes, and sometimes longer. If it fails, wait a little and try again. Do not delete the TXT record later, because Google uses it to keep your ownership verified.

Step 3: Submit your sitemap

In the left menu, open Sitemaps. For a Domain property, type the full address of your sitemap, for example https://example.com/sitemap.xml, and click Submit. If you type only the file name, Search Console may say the address is invalid. After a short time, the status should change to Success, and you will see how many pages Google discovered.

Step 4: Check the Pages report

Open Pages under Indexing. This report shows how many pages are indexed, which means Google has saved them and can show them in search results, and why other pages are not. It can take days or even weeks before all of your pages appear, so be patient with a new site.

Step 5: Inspect one page

Use the search bar at the top to paste the address of a page, such as your home page. The URL inspection tool tells you whether the page is on Google. If it is not, you can click Request indexing. Use this for your most important pages only. You do not need to do it for every page, and asking many times does not make Google work faster.

Quick test

You can also type site:yourdomain.com in Google search. The pages you see are pages Google has indexed. A new site may show only a few results at first.

Common messages and what they mean

- Discovered, currently not indexed: Google knows the page exists but has not visited it yet. Wait and keep improving your site.
- Crawled, currently not indexed: Google visited the page but chose not to add it yet. Check that the content is useful, original, and different from your other pages.
- Not found (404): the address does not exist. Fix the link or remove it from your sitemap.
- Blocked by robots.txt or noindex: something tells Google to stay away. Check your robots.txt file and your page tags.

Tips that really help

- Write helpful, original content instead of copying or mass-producing text
- Link your pages to each other, so that Google can follow the links
- Keep your sitemap up to date when you add posts
- Use clear titles and short descriptions for each page
- Make sure the site loads quickly on a phone

What to expect

Indexing is not guaranteed, and it can take from a few days to several weeks. Appearing in search results is only the first step, because ranking well depends on the quality of your content and many other factors. After a few weeks, open the Performance report to see which searches bring people to your site. Use those ideas to write your next posts.

Final thoughts

Search Console takes about ten minutes to set up and gives you information you cannot get anywhere else. Add your site, verify it, submit your sitemap, and check the reports every few days. Then keep publishing useful content, and let the site grow step by step.
