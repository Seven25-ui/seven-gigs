How to Write an Upwork Proposal That Actually Gets Replies

Nov 5, 2026 · 3 min read

If you are new on Upwork, you have probably sent a proposal and heard nothing back. It happens to everyone. The good news is that most of the competition is sending the same copy-pasted message, so a few small changes can make you stand out.

Here is the structure I use.

1. Start with the client's problem, not with yourself

Do not open with "Hello, my name is... I am a hardworking freelancer with 5 years of experience." The client sees that line 50 times a day. Open with one sentence that shows you read the job post.

Example: "You need a chatbot that answers customer questions from your FAQ and hands off to a human when it is not sure. I have built almost exactly that."

2. Show one relevant piece of proof

Pick one or two portfolio items that match the job. Do not attach everything. If the job is about a support chatbot, attach your chatbot project. A short sentence about what it does is enough. If you have a demo video, even better, because clients can see it working in 30 seconds.

3. Say how you would approach it

Write two or three short sentences about your plan. You do not need to solve the whole job in the proposal. You just need to show that you know where to start.

Example: "I would first load your FAQ into a vector database, connect it to the chat flow, then test it with real questions before handing it over."

4. Keep it short

Aim for 100 to 150 words. Clients read proposals on their phone between meetings. Long walls of text get skipped. If you cannot say it in a few short paragraphs, cut it.

5. Be clear about how you communicate

If you prefer chat only, say it politely in the proposal. Clients appreciate knowing this up front, and it filters out jobs that would not work for you anyway.

6. End with one simple question

Ask something specific about the job, like "Do you already have your FAQ written, or should I help organize it?" A good question makes it easy for the client to reply.

Before you hit send

- Check how many proposals the job already has. Jobs with 50 or more are very crowded, so jobs with fewer than 20 are usually a better use of your Connects.
- Apply early. Jobs posted in the last hour or two have the best chance of being seen.
- Do not boost every proposal. Boosting costs extra Connects, and a good proposal on a good job matters more.
- Read it out loud once. If it sounds like a robot wrote it, rewrite it in your own words.
- Skip jobs you do not really qualify for. A few good proposals beat ten rushed ones.

A simple template you can adjust

"Hi [name], you need [their problem in one line]. I have built [similar project], and you can see it in my portfolio. For your job, I would [step 1], then [step 2], then [step 3]. I work through chat only, so everything stays clear and written down. Quick question: [one specific question about their job]?"

Change the words so it sounds like you. The best proposal is the one that sounds like a real person who is happy to help.

Good luck, and do not get discouraged. Every freelancer starts with zero reviews. Keep sending good proposals, and one reply will change everything.
