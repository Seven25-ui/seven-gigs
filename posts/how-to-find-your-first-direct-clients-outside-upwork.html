How to Find Your First Direct Clients Outside Upwork

Nov 14, 2026 · 4 min read

Platforms like Upwork are a good place to start, but they are not the only place to find work. Many small businesses near you need help and do not know where to look for a freelancer. If you can solve one clear problem for them, you can find direct clients on your own. This guide shows a simple and safe way to do it.

Before you start: respect the platform rules

If you already work with a client through Upwork, keep that work and payment on Upwork. Taking that client away from the platform to avoid fees can break the rules and put your account at risk. This guide is about finding new clients who did not come from a marketplace in the first place.

1. Choose one clear service

Do not tell people you do "everything with computers." Pick one small service that a business can understand in one sentence. Good examples are:

- A chatbot that answers common questions on Messenger or Telegram
- A form that saves new leads into a Google Sheet and sends you an alert
- A simple one-page website with contact details
- A short automation that sends a reminder or a daily report

The clearer the service, the easier it is for a business owner to say yes.

2. Find businesses that have a real problem

Look at small businesses around you: online shops, salons, clinics, repair shops, schools, and restaurants. Check their Facebook pages. Signs that they could use help include slow replies to messages, the same questions answered again and again, no website, or orders written by hand. Make a short list of 10 businesses that really match your service.

3. Start with people you already know

Your first direct client is often a friend, a relative, or a neighbor who owns a small business. Tell them what you can do and ask if they know someone who needs it. People trust people they know, and one happy customer can introduce you to others.

4. Send a short, friendly message

Do not send a long message about yourself. Talk about their problem and offer one specific idea. For example:

"Hi, I saw that customers on your page ask about prices and opening hours a lot. I build small chatbots that answer these questions automatically. Would you like me to make a free demo using your own questions? I work through chat only, so you can reply anytime."

Keep it under 60 words, be polite, and ask one easy question.

5. Make a small demo

A demo is worth more than a long explanation. Build a tiny version using the business's own information, like three questions and answers, and record a short screen video or send a few screenshots. Do not give away the whole project for free. Show enough that the owner can see the value.

6. Write down what is included

Before you start, send a short message that lists what you will deliver, how many changes are included, the price, and the deadline. Ask the client to confirm it in writing. This prevents most misunderstandings.

7. Set a fair price and ask for a deposit

Small businesses care about price, so keep your first offers simple. Break the work into small steps and charge for each step. A common approach is to ask for part of the payment before you begin and the rest when you finish. Accept payment methods that are easy for your client and that leave a record, such as GCash or a bank transfer, and send a simple receipt.

8. Deliver, explain, and ask for feedback

When the work is done, show the client how to use it in a short message or a screen recording. Then ask politely for honest feedback or a short testimonial. With permission, you can add it to your portfolio or your website.

9. Protect yourself

- Never start big work without agreement on the scope and price
- Keep passwords and private data safe, and only use the access you really need
- Do not promise results you cannot control, such as "more sales"
- Keep all conversations in writing

A simple weekly routine

Pick five businesses each week, send five short messages, and build one demo. After a few weeks you will know which messages get replies and which services people want. Do not be discouraged by silence. Many people will not answer, and one yes is enough to start.

Final thoughts

Direct clients take patience, but they can give you steady work, better prices, and long-term relationships. Start small, be clear about what you offer, and treat every client with care. You can keep Upwork as one source of work while you build your own network.
