How to Fact-Check AI Answers Before You Use Them in Client Work

Nov 27, 2026 · 3 min read

AI tools write in a confident, smooth voice, even when the answer is wrong. They can invent a statistic, mix up two tools, or describe a feature that does not exist. For a freelancer, one wrong sentence in a delivered report can hurt trust. The solution is not to stop using AI. It is to add a short checking routine.

1. Mark the claims that matter

Read the answer and underline anything that can be proven wrong: numbers, dates, names, prices, laws, version numbers, links, and step-by-step instructions. Opinions and style suggestions need less checking than facts.

2. Ask for sources, then open them

You can ask the AI where a claim comes from, but never trust the source list without opening it. Some tools produce links or article titles that look real and are not. Open each link yourself and confirm that the page really says what the AI claimed. If you cannot find it, treat the claim as unverified.

3. Cross-check with a second, trusted source

For anything important, find the original: the official documentation, the company website, a government page, or a respected publication. One reliable source is better than three blog posts that repeat each other.

4. Test code before you trust it

Run it on fake data first. AI code may use a function that does not exist, an old version of a library, or a command that behaves differently on your system. Read the code and make sure you understand what it does, especially anything that deletes, renames, or sends data.

5. Be careful with recent information

AI tools may not know about recent changes, such as new prices, new features, or updated rules. For anything that changes often, check the current official page.

6. Watch for the warning signs

Be extra careful when the answer includes very exact numbers with no source, quotes from named people, legal or medical advice, or a perfect-sounding answer to a very specific question. These are the places where made-up details appear most often.

7. Ask the AI to review itself, but do not rely on it

You can ask: "Which parts of your answer are you least sure about?" This sometimes points you to weak spots. But the AI can still be wrong about its own answer, so use this only as a hint about where to look first.

8. Keep a short verified note

When you confirm something, write it down in a simple note with the source link and the date. Next time you need the same fact, you can reuse it without repeating the work.

9. Be honest with clients

If a client asks whether you use AI, answer honestly. You can explain that you use it as a helper and always review and verify the result yourself. Clients care most about correct, useful work.

A quick checklist before you send anything

- Did I check every number, date, and name?
- Did I open every link?
- Did I run and understand every piece of code?
- Does the text sound like me, and do I stand behind it?

AI can save you a lot of time, but you are the one who signs the work. A few minutes of checking is a small price for staying trusted.
