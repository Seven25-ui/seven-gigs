How to Use Your Own Website as a Portfolio When You Apply on Upwork

Nov 16, 2026 · 4 min read

When you are new on Upwork, you may not have many reviews or past projects to show. A simple personal website can help. It gives clients a place to see your work, read how you think, and understand how you communicate. It does not need to be fancy. A free GitHub Pages site is enough to start. This guide explains what to put on it and how to use it carefully.

Always check Upwork's current rules about links and contact information before you add anything to your profile or proposals. The rules can change, and your account is more important than any website.

1. Decide what the site must do

A portfolio site has one job: help a client trust you in a short time. A client should be able to answer these questions in a minute or two:

- What do you do, and for whom?
- Can you show a real example?
- How do you communicate and work?
- How can they contact you?

2. Show two or three strong projects

Quality is better than quantity. Pick two or three projects that match the jobs you want. For each one, create a short page or section with:

- The problem the project solves
- What you built and which tools you used
- A screenshot or a short screen recording
- A link to a live demo, if it is safe to share
- The result, written honestly, without invented numbers

If you do not have client work yet, build your own practice projects, such as a chatbot, an automation workflow, or a small website. Say clearly that they are personal or demo projects.

3. Write a clear About page

Say who you are, what you help with, and how you work. If you prefer written chat, explain it in a friendly way and mention the benefits: clear records, fast replies, and careful updates. Do not share private details that you do not want clients to know.

4. Add a few helpful posts

A small blog shows how you think. Write about problems you solved, tools you learned, or mistakes you made. Posts like these also help people find your site on Google. Keep them honest and useful, and write from your own experience.

5. Make the site easy to use

- It should work well on a phone, because many clients check links on mobile
- Use a clear title and a simple menu
- Keep the pages fast and clean, without too many pop-ups
- Make sure the links and demos really work
- Use HTTPS, and consider your own domain name once you can afford it

6. Add a contact page that matches your style

Include an email address or a simple form. Keep in mind that, for work you find on Upwork, you should keep your conversations and payments on Upwork, according to the platform rules. Your website is for showing your work, not for moving clients away from the platform.

7. Link it where it makes sense

You can add your website to your profile and to portfolio items, if the platform allows it. In a proposal, mention it only when it helps, for example: "I built a similar chatbot, and you can see a short demo on my portfolio." Do not fill your proposals with links, and do not copy the same message every time.

8. Use client words, not only technical words

Many clients are not technical. Instead of listing tools, explain what you did for the user: "The chatbot answers common questions automatically and sends a message to the owner when it cannot help." Clear results make your projects easier to understand.

9. Keep it updated

Remove old or weak projects, and add new ones when you finish them. If you earn a review or a testimonial, ask permission before you show the client's name or company.

10. Be honest

Never claim work you did not do, and never show projects that belong to someone else. A small honest portfolio is better than a large false one.

Final thoughts

A portfolio website will not replace a good proposal, but it supports it. It shows effort, proof, and a clear way of working. Build it step by step, keep it honest, and update it as you grow. Your next client may decide to answer your proposal because of one clear project page.
